# Model card — A3BX3 PBE band-gap models (v2 draft)

Draft, 2026-09-27. Numbers come from `reports/metrics_v2.json` (generated by
`python run_all.py`; every figure below is out-of-fold, never in-sample).

## Intended use

Screening **GGA-PBE band gaps** of A3BX3 inverse-perovskite pnictogen halides
(A ∈ Mg/Ca/Sr/Ba, B ∈ P/As/Sb/Bi, X ∈ F/Cl/Br/I) to decide which compositions are worth a
DFT calculation. It is a ranking and triage aid, not a substitute for DFT.

## Operating limits (binding on every caller)

- **PBE gaps, not real gaps.** PBE underestimates semiconductor gaps, typically by 30–50 %.
  A prediction of 1.3 eV is a PBE-level number. For a beyond-PBE estimate use the separate
  TBmBJ delta model (v1, trained on ABX3 — a cross-stoichiometry transfer).
- **n = 23.** Every CI below is wide. Differences between models smaller than the CI of the
  paired difference are not evidence of anything.
- **Family only.** Not valid outside the 4 × 4 × 4 composition space above, for mixed-halide
  or mixed-cation compositions, or for non-cubic polymorphs. The descriptors are
  compositional; the model cannot tell polymorphs of one formula apart.
- **New A-site cations are not supported.** Under leave-one-A-out no model improves on
  the mean baseline (0.376 eV) by more than 0.11 eV, and both ridges are worse than it. The GPR gains −0.043 eV [−0.095, −0.000] with
  R² 0.004; the best, xgb_magpie, gains −0.108 [−0.193, −0.012]. Do not use any model to
  predict a compound whose A-site is absent from training.
- **Sr3BiX3 holdout, n = 3, cannot rank models.** It is scored once per release and used for
  no choice.
- **SOC unknown** for all 23 training rows (see DATA_CARD). Bi/Sb gaps move with SOC.

## Models and baselines (same folds for all)

Baselines: `mean`, `halide_rule` (per-halide training mean), `ridge_physics9` (as v1),
`ridge_magpie` (VarianceThreshold + scaler + SelectKBest + Ridge, k/alpha by inner grouped CV).
Models: `gpr_physics9` (v1 champion: ARD Matérn 5/2 + white noise), `rf_*`, `xgb_*` on
physics-9 or Magpie, grids chosen by nested grouped CV. Every transform sits inside a
sklearn `Pipeline`.

## Held-out results (MAE eV, bootstrap 95 % CI)

| Split | gpr_physics9 | best other model | mean | ridge_physics9 | GPR − ridge [95 % CI] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Leave-one-B-out (headline)** | **0.146 [0.087, 0.206]** | xgb_physics9 0.223 | 0.329 | 0.195 | −0.049 [−0.109, +0.009] |
| Leave-one-A-out | 0.333 [0.236, 0.439] | xgb_magpie 0.268 | 0.376 | 0.604 | −0.272 [−0.506, −0.074] |
| GroupKFold(5)×10 by formula | 0.217 [0.145, 0.293] | rf_magpie 0.231 | 0.327 | 0.240 | −0.024 [−0.069, +0.025] |
| LOO (v1 comparison) | 0.151 [0.095, 0.211] | xgb_magpie 0.212 | 0.324 | 0.206 | −0.055 [−0.128, +0.010] |

Reading it honestly:

- The GPR beats the **mean** baseline on every split, with CIs on the difference that
  exclude zero on LOBO, GroupKFold and LOO (on LOAO the upper bound is −0.000).
- The GPR does **not** separate from the **ridge** baseline on LOBO, GroupKFold or LOO: the CI
  of the paired difference crosses zero. At n = 23 the evidence is "GPR ≥ a linear fit",
  not "GPR > a linear fit".
- RF and XGB do not beat the GPR on LOBO, LOO or GroupKFold, as pre-registered in the design.
  On leave-one-A-out the Magpie tree models are ahead of the GPR, but every model there is
  close to the mean baseline.
- GroupKFold (≈ 18 training rows per fold) gives 0.217 eV, worse than LOBO's 0.146. The LOBO
  headline is not a worst case; quote it with the GroupKFold number beside it.
- GPR σ calibration (LOBO, out-of-fold): 70 % of points within ±1σ (nominal 68 %), 78 %
  within ±2σ (nominal 95 %). The ±2σ band is **too narrow**; do not treat it as a 95 %
  interval. Conformal intervals are week-6 work.

## Reproduction

v2 `gpr_physics9` reproduces v1 exactly: LOO 0.1512 eV, LOBO 0.1458 eV, Sr3BiX3 0.160 eV
(`v1_reproduction` in the metrics file). `python run_all.py` rebuilds everything offline in
about two minutes; two runs give a byte-identical `metrics_v2.json` (tested).
